Common Water Heater Problems We Fix

  • No Hot Water: Pilot light out, faulty thermostat, broken heating element, or gas supply issues
  • Not Enough Hot Water: Undersized unit, sediment buildup, or thermostat problems
  • Water Too Hot: Thermostat set too high or malfunctioning pressure relief valve
  • Leaking Water Heater: Corroded tank, loose connections, or failed temperature & pressure valve
  • Strange Noises: Sediment buildup causing popping, rumbling, or banging sounds
  • Rusty Water: Corroded anode rod or deteriorating tank interior
  • Smelly Water: Bacteria growth in tank requiring disinfection
  • Pilot Light Won't Stay Lit: Faulty thermocouple, pilot tube, or gas control valve

Signs You Need Water Heater Replacement

Age 10+ Years

Most water heaters last 10-15 years. If yours is approaching this age, replacement may be wise.

Frequent Repairs

Multiple repairs in a short time indicate it's more cost-effective to replace.

Rust or Corrosion

Rusty water or visible rust on the tank means internal corrosion and impending failure.

Leaking Tank

Water pooling around the heater indicates tank failure. Replacement is necessary.

Inadequate Hot Water

If repairs don't solve hot water shortage, you may need a larger or more efficient unit.

Rising Energy Bills

Old, inefficient water heaters waste energy. New models save significantly on utilities.
